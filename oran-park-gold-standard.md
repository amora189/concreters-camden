## MODULE 4 — Ground conditions
**Widget:** `e-heading` + `text-editor` (~200 words) + `image` ×2 — **UNIQUE**

### H2
`Wianamatta clay and engineered fill`

### Body
> Oran Park sits on Wianamatta Group shale, and the residual soil above it is reactive clay — it swells when it's wet and shrinks when it dries, moving through the seasons underneath whatever you pour on it. Across the Camden growth suburbs that typically classifies M to H1 under AS 2870, the Australian standard for residential slabs and footings on reactive soils, with H2 showing up in some profiles.
>
> The Oran Park wrinkle is engineered fill. Most of these lots were shaped at subdivision stage — cut here, filled there, compacted, and then left for a year or two before a house went on them. By the time you're pouring a driveway or a rear-yard shed slab, you're often working over fill that was placed and tested for the house footprint, not necessarily for where the shed is going.
>
> That's not a reason to panic and it's not a reason to over-engineer. It's a reason to check before pouring rather than assume, because a shed slab that settles differentially over soft fill cracks in a way no amount of mesh prevents.

## MODULE 5 — Ground preparation
**Widget:** `e-heading` + `text-editor` (~150 words) — **UNIQUE**

### H2
`How we prepare the ground in Oran Park`

### Body
> On a handover lot the driveway area usually still has builder's spoil, offcuts and a compacted haul route through it. That comes out first. We excavate to depth, identify anything soft or organic, and remove it rather than covering it over.
>
> Then a compacted crushed-rock base goes in, so the slab sits on uniform support instead of a mix of undisturbed clay and loose fill. That step matters most under the driveway and garage approach, where vehicle loads concentrate.
>
> Reinforcement goes in on bar chairs so the steel sits inside the slab rather than lying on the dirt — mesh on the ground does almost nothing. Camden Council specifies SL72 fabric in a 125mm slab at 32 MPa for a residential crossing, and that's the standard we build the whole driveway to, not just the section on council land.

## MODULE 6 — Drainage, levels and crack control
**Widget:** `e-heading` + `text-editor` (~200 words) + `image` ×2 — **REWRITE PER SUBURB**

### H2
`Levels, falls and where the water goes`

### Body
> On a flat estate lot with a fence 900mm off the garage wall, the driveway and side path decide where every drop of roof and surface water ends up. Set the falls wrong and it ponds against the garage threshold or pushes water at the neighbour's fence line.
>
> Camden also has a specific requirement worth knowing: where there's no concrete footpath in front of your property yet, the footpath allocation area has to be graded at 4% crossfall, so a footpath can be built later without an awkward step. If that grade can't be achieved, Council wants a pre-commencement inspection before you pour. Batters across the footpath can't be steeper than 1 in 6.
>
> Concrete also shrinks as it cures and keeps moving with the ground. Reinforcement holds a slab together; it doesn't make it crack-proof — nothing does. Control joints cut to about a quarter of the slab depth give that shrinkage somewhere tidy to go. We place them at internal corners, the garage entrance, changes in slab width, and where new concrete meets existing.

## MODULE 7 — Council crossovers
**Widget:** `e-heading` + `text-editor` (~150 words) — **UNIQUE**

### H2
`Camden Council crossovers in Oran Park`

### Body
> Any driveway that crosses council land needs Camden Council approval before it's poured. For a standard job that's the Standard Residential Driveway Crossing Application; where the site can't meet the standard specification — a steep grade, an awkward corner lot, a commercial access — it's the Non-Standard Driveway Application instead, with an explanation of why.
>
> Camden's specification for an urban residential crossing is 4.0m minimum to 5.5m maximum width, 125mm thick, 32 MPa concrete, SL72 reinforcing fabric. The footpath allocation is 1200mm wide and, in Oran Park specifically, starts 800mm from your property boundary rather than the 900mm that applies elsewhere in the LGA.
>
> Licence and public liability insurance details aren't required at lodgement but must be with Council before work starts. We handle the application and the inspections as part of the job so you're not chasing Council yourself.

### Q1 — `Why is the footpath set-out different in Oran Park?`
> Camden Council's driveway specification puts the footpath allocation 900mm back from the property boundary across most of the LGA, but 800mm within Oran Park. The allocation itself is 1200mm wide either way. It's a small difference that matters at set-out, because the crossover has to line up with where a future footpath will actually be built.

Fill that's fine under a house pad can be softer where a shed is going. We check the ground before quoting rather than pouring and hoping.

## Register notes for Codex

Read the Werribee source copy and match this voice:

- Second person, direct. "Get the crossover set-out wrong and it fails inspection", not "It is essential that crossover set-out is performed correctly."
- Concede limits. "Reinforcement holds a slab together; it doesn't make it crack-proof — nothing does." Honesty reads as competence.
- Numbers over adjectives. "125mm at 32 MPa with SL72" beats "built to the highest standards".
- No estate-agent language. No "nestled", "boasts", "premier", "your dream driveway".
- Short paragraphs, two to four sentences.
- Australian English: metre, colour, specialise, kerb.
- Never claim volume you don't have. No "hundreds of Oran Park driveways".
